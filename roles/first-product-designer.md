# How to hire your first product designer

## When to hire

Hire your first product designer when engineers are deciding how the product looks and works by default, and customers are starting to notice.

## The role in one sentence

> We need a founding product designer: has shipped complex web application screens, works directly with engineers, and can do research and visual design alone.

## Job description template

[Company] is building [product]. You would be the first designer: you decide how the product works and looks, from rough flows to finished screens, and you work with the engineers who build it the same day.

**What you will do**

- Design flows and screens for new features from problem to shipped detail.
- Talk to users and watch them use the product.
- Build and keep a small design system the engineers actually use.
- Work in short loops with engineers; review what ships.
- Own the look of the product and the brand basics.

**What we look for**

- A portfolio with shipped product work, with your part made clear.
- You have designed dense application screens: tables, settings, workflows.
- You are comfortable doing research, interaction and visual design yourself.
- You can explain why a design is the way it is.

Add the location, the pay range and equity, and how the interview process runs.

## Screening brief

**Must-haves**

- A portfolio with shipped product work, not only concepts, because the job is shipping.
- Has designed complex application screens, because that is what the product is.
- Has worked directly with engineers without a product manager in between, because there is none.

**Either of these profiles works**

- Designer at an early-stage startup who covered everything.
- Designer from a strong product team who owned a product area end to end.

**Bonuses**

- Writes front-end code or prototypes in code.
- Has built a design system.
- Brand and marketing design skills.

## Interview plan

| Stage | What it tests |
|---|---|
| Intro call (30 min, founder) | What they have shipped, what they want to do next. |
| Portfolio review (60 min) | Two projects in depth: the problem, the options they rejected, what shipped and what happened after. |
| Working session (90 min) | Work through a real problem from your product together. Tests thinking and collaboration, not polish. |
| Team conversation and references (60 min) | With an engineer: how they hand off, review and disagree. |

## Scorecard

Rate each criterion from 1 to 4 with a written example.

| Criterion | What strong looks like |
|---|---|
| Problem framing | Starts with who the user is and what they are trying to do. |
| Interaction design | Handles states, errors and edge cases, not just the main screen. |
| Visual craft | Clear hierarchy, consistent spacing and type in shipped work. |
| Works with engineers | Designs with the build cost in mind and reviews the result. |
| Reasoning | Can explain and defend choices, and change them given a good argument. |

## Interview questions

- Show me something you shipped. What did the first version look like, and why did it change?
- Tell me about a design that tested badly or failed after launch.
- How do you decide what not to design?
- What would you change first in our product, and what would you want to learn before changing it?
- How do you work with an engineer who disagrees with your design?

## Common mistakes

- Judging the portfolio by how pretty it is instead of what shipped.
- Unpaid take-home redesigns of your product.
- Hiring a brand designer for a product design job, or the reverse.
