# How to hire a founding engineer

## When to hire

Hire a founding engineer when the product needs more building than the founders can do, and there is enough clarity about the first customers to say what to build for the next six months.

## The role in one sentence

> We need a founding engineer: full-stack, TypeScript and Postgres, in New York, who has shipped a product end to end.

## Job description template

[Company] is building [one line about the product]. We are [N] people and you would be the first engineer after the founders. You will own large parts of the product from the first line of code to production, and help decide what we build.

**What you will do**

- Build and ship customer-facing features end to end, from data model to interface.
- Make the early technical decisions and keep them simple enough to change later.
- Talk to customers and turn what you hear into what gets built next.
- Set up the basics: deploys, monitoring, tests where they pay off.
- Help hire the next engineers.

**What we look for**

- You have shipped a product or a major feature end to end and can show it.
- You are comfortable across the stack: [your stack].
- You have worked at an early-stage company or built something of your own.
- You make reasonable decisions with incomplete information and say when you are unsure.

Add the location, the pay range and equity, and how the interview process runs.

## Screening brief

**Must-haves**

- Has shipped a product or major feature end to end, because there is nobody to hand parts off to.
- Hands-on with a comparable stack in the last two years, because the first months are all building.
- Has worked where requirements were unclear (early startup, own project, zero-to-one team), because nobody will write tickets.

**Either of these profiles works**

- Senior engineer from a startup who built a product area alone.
- Engineer from a larger company with a serious side project or open-source work that shows range.

**Bonuses**

- Has talked to customers directly.
- Has hired or mentored engineers.
- Knows your domain.

## Interview plan

| Stage | What it tests |
|---|---|
| Intro call (30 min, founder) | Motivation, what they have built, whether the stage and pay range fit. |
| Technical deep dive (60 min) | One project they built: decisions, trade-offs, what they would change. Tests depth and honesty. |
| Working session (2–3 hours, paid if longer) | Build a small real feature together in your codebase or a realistic one. Tests how they work, not puzzle skill. |
| Founder conversation and references (60 min) | How they decide, disagree and handle unclear goals. Two references from people who worked with them closely. |

## Scorecard

Rate each criterion from 1 to 4 with a written example.

| Criterion | What strong looks like |
|---|---|
| Builds end to end | Has shipped whole features alone and can explain every layer. |
| Judgment | Chooses the simple option, names trade-offs, knows what to skip for now. |
| Product sense | Asks who the user is and what problem is solved before designing. |
| Ownership | Describes following problems through to production and after. |
| Communication | Explains technical decisions clearly to a non-engineer. |

## Interview questions

- Walk me through something you built from nothing to production. What was the first version, and what did you leave out?
- Tell me about a technical decision you made that turned out wrong. How did you find out?
- What would you build first here, and what would you want to know before starting?
- When did you last disagree with a product decision? What happened?
- What do you do when nobody tells you what to work on?

## Common mistakes

- Testing algorithm puzzles when the job is building product.
- Hiring for the scale you hope to have instead of the product you need to ship.
- Asking for ten technologies. Name the two that matter.
