# How to hire your first operations manager

## When to hire

Hire your first operations manager when founders spend hours a week on finance, legal, payroll, vendors and people admin, and mistakes are starting to cost money.

## The role in one sentence

> We need a first operations hire: has run finance, people and legal basics at a startup of 10 to 50 people, organized, and happy to do the work without a team.

## Job description template

[Company] is [N] people. You would be the first operations hire and take finance, people operations, legal and office basics off the founders, working with outside accountants and lawyers.

**What you will do**

- Run monthly books, invoicing and payroll with the accountant.
- Handle contracts, vendors and compliance with outside counsel.
- Own onboarding, benefits and people admin.
- Keep the budget and cash forecast.
- Set up simple processes and tools that fit the company's size.

**What we look for**

- You have run several of these areas at a small company.
- You are careful with details and deadlines.
- You know when to ask an accountant or lawyer.
- You are discreet with salaries and personal data.

Add the location, the pay range and equity, and how the interview process runs.

## Screening brief

**Must-haves**

- Has owned finance or people operations at a company of a similar size, because the work starts on day one.
- Has worked with outside accountants, payroll providers or lawyers, because that is how the work gets done at this size.
- Careful and reliable, confirmed by references, because errors here are costly.

**Either of these profiles works**

- Operations or business manager at a startup.
- Finance or HR generalist from a small company who wants a broader role.

**Bonuses**

- Has been through a fundraise or an audit.
- Has set up payroll in more than one country.
- Has chosen and rolled out finance or HR tools.

## Interview plan

| Stage | What it tests |
|---|---|
| Intro call (30 min, founder) | What they have run, at what company size. |
| Experience review (60 min) | Concrete walk-through: a month-end close, an onboarding, a contract they handled. |
| Practical exercise (60 min) | A small real task: review a budget sheet for errors, or plan the first 60 days. |
| Founder conversation and references (60 min) | References from a founder or manager who relied on them. |

## Scorecard

Rate each criterion from 1 to 4 with a written example.

| Criterion | What strong looks like |
|---|---|
| Breadth | Has done finance, people and legal basics hands-on. |
| Accuracy | Catches errors in the exercise and explains their checks. |
| Judgment | Knows what to handle and what to send to a specialist. |
| Process sense | Builds the lightest process that works and no more. |
| Trust | References confirm reliability and discretion. |

## Interview questions

- Walk me through a month-end close you ran. What usually went wrong?
- Tell me about a mistake you caught before it became expensive.
- What did you set up from nothing at your last company?
- When did you last call a lawyer or accountant, and why?
- What would you look at in your first two weeks here?

## Common mistakes

- Hiring a senior CFO or COO for work that is mostly doing.
- Combining the role with executive assistant work without saying so.
- Skipping reference checks for a role with access to money and personal data.
