# How to hire your first customer success manager

## When to hire

Hire your first customer success manager when founders spend a large part of the week on onboarding and support, or customers are churning for reasons that better onboarding would fix.

## The role in one sentence

> We need a first customer success manager: B2B SaaS, has onboarded and renewed accounts alone, technical enough to solve problems without an engineer.

## Job description template

[Company] has [N] customers using [product]. You would be the first customer success hire: you onboard new customers, keep them successful, handle support, and make sure the team hears what customers need.

**What you will do**

- Onboard every new customer to first value.
- Answer support questions and solve what you can yourself.
- Watch usage and act before a customer goes quiet.
- Own renewals and spot expansion.
- Write the help docs and bring patterns back to product.

**What we look for**

- You have owned a book of B2B accounts through onboarding and renewal.
- You can investigate a technical problem and explain it simply.
- You have worked where there was no process and built one.
- You write well; much of the job is written.

Add the location, the pay range and equity, and how the interview process runs.

## Screening brief

**Must-haves**

- Has onboarded and renewed B2B customers as the responsible person, because that is the core of the job.
- Can solve product problems without escalating everything, because engineers are few.
- Clear writer, shown in the application and a sample, because support and docs are written.

**Either of these profiles works**

- Customer success or account manager at an early-stage SaaS company.
- Support or solutions engineer who wants to own customer relationships.

**Bonuses**

- Knows your customers' industry.
- Has written help-center content.
- Has handled expansion or upsell.

## Interview plan

| Stage | What it tests |
|---|---|
| Intro call (30 min, founder) | Accounts they have managed, numbers, why this stage. |
| Account review (45 min) | One customer they saved and one they lost, in detail. |
| Practical exercise (60 min) | Reply in writing to two real support messages, then run a short mock onboarding call. |
| Team conversation and references (45 min) | With an engineer: how they report bugs and push for fixes. |

## Scorecard

Rate each criterion from 1 to 4 with a written example.

| Criterion | What strong looks like |
|---|---|
| Customer understanding | Finds out what the customer is trying to achieve, not just what they asked. |
| Problem solving | Investigates before escalating; reports issues precisely. |
| Writing | Replies are short, clear and kind. |
| Ownership of outcomes | Talks about retention and results, not activity. |
| Product feedback | Turns many requests into a clear pattern for product. |

## Interview questions

- Tell me about a customer who was about to leave. What did you do?
- Describe an onboarding that went badly. What did you change afterwards?
- How do you decide which customers need attention this week?
- Tell me about a bug you tracked down yourself.
- What did you convince a product team to build, and how?

## Common mistakes

- Treating the role as pure support and hiring only for patience.
- Hiring before you know what a successful customer looks like.
- Giving the hire a revenue target without the authority to fix what causes churn.
