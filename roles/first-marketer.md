# How to hire your first marketer

## When to hire

Hire your first marketer when there is a product people keep using and at least one way of finding customers that works a little, so marketing has something to scale.

## The role in one sentence

> We need a first marketer: B2B SaaS, writes well, has grown one channel from nothing to a real share of pipeline, and does the work alone.

## Job description template

[Company] sells [product] to [customer]. You would be the first marketing hire. You find out which channels can bring us customers, do the work yourself, and measure what it produces.

**What you will do**

- Sharpen who we sell to and how we describe the product.
- Pick two or three channels, run them yourself and drop what does not work.
- Write: website, launch posts, emails, case studies.
- Set up simple measurement from first touch to revenue.
- Work with the founders and sales on what buyers ask.

**What we look for**

- You have grown at least one channel from zero to meaningful pipeline or sign-ups, and can show the numbers.
- You write clearly without an agency or an editor.
- You have worked at an early-stage B2B company.
- You are comfortable with numbers and with being wrong quickly.

Add the location, the pay range and equity, and how the interview process runs.

## Screening brief

**Must-haves**

- Has personally built one channel that produced measurable pipeline or sign-ups, because there is no team to direct.
- Strong writer, shown by samples, because most early marketing is writing.
- Has worked in B2B with a similar buyer or deal size, because consumer tactics rarely transfer.

**Either of these profiles works**

- Generalist who was marketer number one or two at a startup.
- Specialist in content, product marketing or demand generation with visible range.

**Bonuses**

- Has done launches.
- Can build pages and automations without engineering help.
- Knows your buyer.

## Interview plan

| Stage | What it tests |
|---|---|
| Intro call (30 min, founder) | What they built, with numbers. What they want to own. |
| Work review (60 min) | Two pieces of work in depth: the goal, what they did themselves, the result. |
| Written exercise (paid, 2 hours) | A one-page plan for your first 90 days, or a rewrite of one page of your site. Tests thinking and writing. |
| Founder conversation and references (60 min) | How they choose what to do, and what they stop doing. |

## Scorecard

Rate each criterion from 1 to 4 with a written example.

| Criterion | What strong looks like |
|---|---|
| Positioning | Can say who the product is for and why, in plain words. |
| Writing | Samples are clear, specific and free of filler. |
| Channel skill | Deep, hands-on knowledge of at least one channel. |
| Measurement | Ties work to pipeline or revenue and knows the limits of the data. |
| Prioritization | Proposes a small number of bets and explains what they would skip. |

## Interview questions

- Which channel did you build from nothing? What were the numbers at the start and a year later?
- Show me something you wrote that worked, and something that did not.
- How would you describe our product to a buyer in two sentences?
- What would you do in your first 30 days here, and what would you not do?
- Tell me about a campaign you stopped. How did you decide?

## Common mistakes

- Hiring a head of marketing who has only managed teams and agencies.
- Expecting one person to cover brand, content, paid, events and product marketing.
- Hiring before you know who the customer is.
