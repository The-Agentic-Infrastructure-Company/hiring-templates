# How to hire your first salesperson

## When to hire

Hire your first salesperson after the founders have closed the first customers themselves and can describe who buys, why, and what the sales conversation looks like.

## The role in one sentence

> We need a founding account executive: B2B SaaS, full sales cycle, deals of $10–50k, who has sold at a company before it had a playbook.

## Job description template

[Company] sells [product] to [customer]. The founders have closed the first [N] customers. You would be the first sales hire: you take over the full cycle from first call to signed contract, and you write down what works so the next hires can repeat it.

**What you will do**

- Run the full sales cycle: outreach, discovery, demo, proposal, close.
- Build pipeline yourself; there is no SDR team yet.
- Bring what you hear from buyers back to product every week.
- Turn what works into a written playbook.
- Keep the CRM accurate enough to forecast from.

**What we look for**

- You have carried and hit a quota selling B2B software with similar deal sizes.
- You have built your own pipeline, not only worked inbound leads.
- You have sold a product that was new or unknown.
- You write clearly and follow up without being asked.

Add the location, the pay range and equity, and how the interview process runs.

## Screening brief

**Must-haves**

- Has closed B2B deals of a similar size and cycle as an individual seller, because that is the whole job in year one.
- Has generated own pipeline, because nobody will hand over leads.
- Has sold at an early-stage company or a new product line, because there is no brand or playbook to lean on.

**Either of these profiles works**

- Account executive at a startup from early stage through growth.
- Top seller at a larger company who launched a new product or territory.

**Bonuses**

- Has sold to your buyer persona.
- Has written a sales playbook.
- Is technical enough to demo alone.

## Interview plan

| Stage | What it tests |
|---|---|
| Intro call (30 min, founder) | Their numbers, deal sizes and why they want an early stage. |
| Deal review (45 min) | Two real deals in detail, one won and one lost. Tests whether they understand why deals close. |
| Mock discovery call (45 min) | They run discovery with a founder playing a buyer, after a short brief on your product. Tests questions and listening, not product knowledge. |
| Plan and references (60 min) | Their plan for the first 90 days. References from a former manager and a customer if possible. |

## Scorecard

Rate each criterion from 1 to 4 with a written example.

| Criterion | What strong looks like |
|---|---|
| Discovery | Asks about the buyer's problem and process before pitching. |
| Pipeline generation | Can describe exactly how they found and opened their last deals. |
| Closing discipline | Knows the next step, the decision maker and the risk in every deal. |
| Learning speed | Picked up your product from a short brief and used it sensibly. |
| Honesty about numbers | Explains quota, attainment and misses without spin. |

## Interview questions

- Take me through the last deal you closed from the first touch. What nearly killed it?
- Tell me about a deal you lost that you should have won.
- Where did your pipeline come from last quarter, in percentages?
- How would you find our first twenty prospects next week?
- What did you learn about the product from customers that your company acted on?

## Common mistakes

- Hiring a VP of Sales when you need someone who sells.
- Hiring before the founders have closed deals themselves.
- Being impressed by a big-company logo where leads arrived on their own.
